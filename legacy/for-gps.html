For general practitioners

A referral should take a minute.

You identify the patient and write the referral. We handle contact, scheduling, travel, the interview and the report — and every prescribing decision comes back to you.

Who to refer

Consider a review when…

There is no fixed checklist — you assess whether the patient would benefit. In practice, these are the patterns that most often turn up something worth acting on.

  • They take a number of medicines regularly, particularly five or more
  • Medicines are coming from several doctors or specialists
  • They are starting a new medicine, or one with a narrow therapeutic index
  • They have recently been discharged from hospital
  • They report feeling unwell after taking their medicines
  • Doses are being missed, doubled or confused
  • The patient or their family is simply worried about the medicines

What to include

Four things, and we can start.

Anything beyond this is welcome but not required — we'll come back to you if something essential is missing.

Patient details

Name, date of birth, address and the best contact number for arranging the visit.

Current medicines

Your current list, however it prints from your software.

Relevant history

Conditions, allergies, recent admissions and recent pathology where relevant.

Reason for referral

One line is plenty — it tells the pharmacist what to look hardest at.

The report

Written to be actioned, not filed.

Findings are prioritised by clinical risk and written in the order you would want to raise them. Reasoning and references sit underneath each recommendation, so you can accept, modify or set aside any of it in seconds.

Recommendations are advisory. Nexus Pharmacy Consultancy does not prescribe, and no change is made to a patient's regimen without your decision.

Reconciled list

What the patient is actually taking, against what is on file.

Risk findings

Recommendations

Specific, prioritised, and justified — with the evidence cited.

Patient copy
