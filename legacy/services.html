What we do

Medication review, done properly.

Four services, one principle: the only way to know what a patient is really taking is to sit with them and look.

Service 01

Home Medicines Review

A credentialed pharmacist visits the patient in their own home and reviews every medicine they take — checking that each one is safe, is being taken correctly, and is still working for them.

It is a different exercise from a review in your rooms. At home we can see the dose administration aid, the duplicate boxes, the inhaler technique, the expired cream at the back of the drawer, and the note on the fridge that explains why a dose keeps getting missed.

Refer for an HMR

What the pharmacist checks

Everything in the house that the patient takes or applies — not just what's on the script list.

  • Prescription medicines, including those from other prescribers
  • Over-the-counter products, vitamins, supplements and herbal remedies
  • Inhalers, patches, creams, eye and ear drops
  • Expiry dates, storage conditions and leftover stock
  • Device technique — inhalers, blood pressure and glucose monitors

Service 02

Residential Medication Management Review

The equivalent service for residents of aged care homes, where a doctor's referral is required.

The pharmacist attends the facility and discusses the resident's medicines with them and, where appropriate, their family, carers and nursing staff. The report goes back to the GP and the care team together, with practical suggestions that account for how medicines are actually administered on the floor.

Talk to us about facility coverage

Resident-centred

The resident is part of the conversation wherever they are able to be — not reviewed at a distance from a chart.

Team-aware

Recommendations consider nursing routines, swallowing difficulties, PRN use and administration rounds.

Service 03

Post-discharge reconciliation.

The fortnight after a hospital stay is when medicine lists and reality diverge the fastest. Doses change, brands change, and the old box is usually still in the cupboard. We prioritise these referrals.

Discharge summary

We start from what the hospital sent, alongside the pre-admission list held by the practice.

Home check

Every container in the house is matched against both lists, including what was stopped.

Discrepancies

Duplications, omissions and dose mismatches are flagged clearly, with the likely cause.

Back to you

A short, prioritised report so the next consult starts from an accurate list.

Service 04

Medication Management Plans & education.

After the GP has discussed the report with the patient, the patient receives a Medication Management Plan — a plain-language summary they can share with any healthcare provider. With their agreement, a copy also goes to their local pharmacy.

What patients receive

Reminder strategies

Practical systems that fit the household — not a generic pamphlet.

Device coaching

Inhalers, monitors and injectables, demonstrated and checked back.

Carer briefing

Family and carers included, with their questions answered directly.

One shareable plan

A single document the patient can hand to any clinician they see.
