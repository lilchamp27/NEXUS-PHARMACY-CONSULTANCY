Why it matters

Supporting health,
preventing harm.

Medicine-related problems are one of the most preventable causes of hospital admission in Australia — and they rarely show up in a consulting room. They show up at home: in the second box of the same tablet, the expired inhaler, the dose that gets skipped because it makes someone dizzy.

See how a review works

What's prescribed isn't always what's taken

Lists drift. Old boxes stay in the cupboard, specialists add and subtract, and over-the-counter products never make it onto the file. A home visit reconciles the list against reality.

Interactions build quietly

Sedation, falls, bleeding risk, renal strain — the risk of interaction climbs with every added medicine, and often presents as something easy to mistake for ageing.

Small adjustments, meaningful results

Most of what a review finds is fixable: a timing change, a duplication removed, a device used correctly, a plan the patient finally understands.

Our services

Clinical review, end to end.

Each service is delivered by a credentialed consultant pharmacist and reported back to the referring practitioner in a form you can act on at the next consultation.

01

Home Medicines Review

HMR · In the patient's home

A credentialed pharmacist visits the patient at home and reviews every medicine they take — prescription, over-the-counter, vitamins, supplements, herbal remedies, inhalers, patches, creams and drops. Expiry dates, storage, technique and adherence are all checked, and a written report goes back to the referring GP.

02

Residential Medication Management Review

RMMR · Aged care

For residents of aged care homes. The pharmacist attends the facility, discusses the resident's medicines with them, their family, carers and nursing staff, then reports to the GP and care team with practical suggestions for improvement.

03

Post-discharge medication reconciliation

Transitions of care

The days after a hospital stay are the highest-risk window in a patient's year. We prioritise recently discharged patients, reconciling the discharge summary against what is actually in the house and flagging discrepancies early.

04

Medication Management Plan & education

Patient-facing

After your follow-up consultation, the patient receives a plain-language Medication Management Plan they can share with any healthcare provider — plus a one-to-one education session covering devices, timing and reminder strategies.

The pathway

From referral to resolution.

Four steps, with the GP in control at the start and the end.

Referral

The GP assesses that the patient would benefit and sends a referral with a current medicines list and relevant history.

Home visit

We contact the patient, arrange a time that suits them, and a credentialed pharmacist conducts the interview in their home.

Report

A structured report with findings and clear recommendations is written and sent back to the referring GP.

Follow-up

The GP reviews the report with the patient and agrees a Medication Management Plan. With consent, a copy goes to their pharmacy.
