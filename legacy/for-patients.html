Home/For Patients

For patients & carers

Someone to go through your medicines with you.

A Home Medicines Review is a free visit from a credentialed pharmacist who checks that your medicines are safe, that you're taking them correctly, and that they're working for you.

What it is

A conversation, at your kitchen table.

Your doctor refers you. A pharmacist comes to your home at a time that suits you, and goes through everything you take — one medicine at a time, at your pace, with as many questions as you want to ask.

Your community pharmacy is part of the process too, and your carer, family member or community nurse is welcome to sit in. It is different from the review your doctor does in their rooms: that one looks at whether you're on the right medicines for your condition, while this one looks at how those medicines work in your actual day.

Who it's for

It might be worth asking your doctor if…

There's no strict list. Your doctor decides whether you'd benefit — but these are good reasons to raise it.

You take a lot of medicines

Especially five or more regularly, or medicines that have been added one at a time over the years.

They come from different doctors

When a GP, a specialist and a hospital are all prescribing, nobody automatically sees the whole picture.

You've just left hospital

Doses and brands often change during an admission, and the old boxes are usually still at home.

Something new has started

A new medicine is the most common moment for an unexpected interaction or side effect.

You feel unwell after taking them

Dizziness, drowsiness, nausea or falls are worth investigating, not putting up with.

You forget doses — or you're worried

Both are common, both are fixable, and neither is anything to be embarrassed about.

What happens

Before, during and after.

Before the visit

Gather everything you take and put it on the table — prescription medicines, anything you buy over the counter, vitamins, supplements, herbal remedies, and any inhalers, patches, creams, or eye and ear drops. Bring the actual boxes and bottles rather than a list.

During the visit

The pharmacist goes through each medicine with you — what it's for, how and when you take it, whether it's still in date, and how it's stored. They'll check how you use any devices, and talk through ways to remember doses that actually fit your routine.

After the visit

The pharmacist writes a report and sends it to your doctor. Book a follow-up appointment — your doctor will go through the report with you and may change some medicines. You'll receive a Medication Management Plan you can share with any health professional you see.

In aged care

If you live in an aged care home.

The service is called a Residential Medication Management Review, and your doctor needs to refer you for it.

The pharmacist visits you at the home and talks about your medicines with you and — if you'd like — your family, carers and the staff who look after you. They then report back to your doctor and your care team with suggestions for improving how your medicines are managed.

You don't pay for the Home Medicines Review. You may still pay for the appointment with your doctor where the referral is written, unless your doctor bulk bills.
